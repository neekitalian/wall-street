## Institutional roles

| Actor | Core activity | Typical revenue | Risk retained or transferred |
|---|---|---|---|
| Investment bank | Advise, underwrite, distribute, finance | Advisory and underwriting fees, spread | Execution, inventory, reputation, underwriting |
| Sales | Match products and clients | Revenue credit or commission | Suitability and relationship risk; usually little market risk |
| Trader | Price and manage inventory | Bid-ask spread and trading P&L | Market, basis, liquidity, funding |
| Market maker | Quote both sides and supply immediacy | Spread, rebates, inventory management | Adverse selection and inventory |
| Private equity sponsor | Buy control and improve or reprice a company | Management fee, carried interest, capital gain | Equity and reputational risk; debt sits largely at portfolio company |
| Hedge fund | Express a strategy with flexible instruments | Management fee and performance fee | Strategy, leverage, liquidity, counterparty |
| Distressed fund | Buy impaired claims and negotiate recovery | Discount-to-recovery gain, control value | Legal, valuation, timing, liquidity |
| Lender or bondholder | Supply debt capital | Interest, fees, recovery | Default, duration, covenant and collateral risk |
| Management | Operate the company | Salary, bonus, equity | Employment and concentrated equity exposure |
| Board | Govern and approve major actions | Usually fixed compensation | Fiduciary and oversight risk |

Always test the agency problem: the institution, employee, client, investor, and portfolio company may have different incentives.

## Transaction loops

### Sales and trading

`client demand -> sales coverage -> trader quote -> execution -> inventory hedge -> spread/P&L -> compensation`

Ask whether the bank is transferring inventory, creating liquidity, underwriting risk, or taking a proprietary view.

### Leveraged buyout

`target cash flow -> debt capacity -> sponsor equity + acquisition debt -> control -> operational/financial changes -> debt paydown -> exit`

Value creation may come from growth, margin improvement, multiple expansion, leverage, tax effects, or asset sales. Keep these sources separate.

### Hedge-fund trade

`thesis -> instrument -> position size -> financing -> hedge -> catalyst -> path risk -> exit`

A correct thesis can still lose because of timing, carry, margin calls, crowded positioning, or counterparty failure.

### Distressed restructuring

`enterprise value -> legal entities -> claims and collateral -> priority -> creditor groups -> valuation contest -> plan/settlement -> recovery or control`

Map assets and liabilities by legal entity. A consolidated balance sheet can hide structural subordination and guarantees.

## Capital structure and control

Analyze both economic priority and legal rights:

1. superpriority or debtor-in-possession financing;
2. secured senior debt;
3. unsecured senior debt;
4. subordinated or junior debt;
5. preferred equity;
6. common equity.

Actual outcomes depend on collateral, guarantees, covenants, intercreditor agreements, fraudulent-transfer claims, executory contracts, jurisdiction, and negotiated settlements. Do not treat the list as an automatic recovery waterfall.

Control can move without common-equity ownership through covenants, defaults, collateral enforcement, consent thresholds, board designation rights, or a restructuring plan.

## Failure modes

- Market: the asset moves against the position.
- Basis: related prices stop converging.
- Funding: lenders withdraw or reprice financing.
- Liquidity: exit size exceeds market depth.
- Collateral: margin calls force sales before the thesis resolves.
- Counterparty: the other side cannot perform.
- Model: assumptions, data, correlations, or regime estimates fail.
- Legal: priority or transaction validity differs from expectations.
- Governance: agents optimize compensation or control rather than enterprise value.
- Reflexivity: forced actions worsen the price and trigger more forced actions.
