## Canonical route

Use this default order for a reader who wants institutional finance rather than stock selection:

1. *Liar's Poker* - sales, trading, bonds, incentives, and internal politics.
2. *Barbarians at the Gate* - LBO financing, control, boards, advisers, and auctions.
3. *Den of Thieves* - merger arbitrage, information networks, enforcement, and legal boundaries.
4. *More Money Than God* - taxonomy and history of hedge-fund strategies.
5. *When Genius Failed* - relative-value arbitrage, leverage, liquidity, and systemic rescue.
6. *The Big Short* - discovering mispricing and finding instruments and counterparties to express it.
7. *The Man Who Solved the Market* - data, signals, portfolio construction, leverage, and execution as a system.
8. *The Caesars Palace Coup* - legal entities, claim priority, distressed investors, and restructuring warfare.

Add *The Predators' Ball* after *Barbarians at the Gate* when available to connect junk bonds, Drexel, hostile takeovers, and corporate raiders.

## Compressed route

For limited time, use:

1. *Liar's Poker*
2. *Barbarians at the Gate*
3. selected strategy chapters from *More Money Than God*
4. *When Genius Failed*
5. *The Big Short*
6. *The Caesars Palace Coup*

Treat *Den of Thieves* and *The Man Who Solved the Market* as specialist branches rather than prerequisites.

## Goal-specific routes

### Investment banking and private equity

`Liar's Poker -> Barbarians at the Gate -> The Predators' Ball -> The Caesars Palace Coup`

Track fees, financing certainty, board duties, management incentives, debt capacity, and exit assumptions.

### Hedge funds and trading

`Liar's Poker -> More Money Than God -> When Genius Failed -> The Big Short -> The Man Who Solved the Market`

Track thesis, instrument, position sizing, financing, liquidity, catalyst, path dependency, and exit.

### Distressed debt and restructuring

`Barbarians at the Gate -> When Genius Failed -> The Caesars Palace Coup`

Add a basic accounting or bankruptcy-law reference if the reader cannot distinguish enterprise value, equity value, secured claims, guarantees, and legal entities.

### Quantitative finance

`When Genius Failed -> The Man Who Solved the Market`

Emphasize that both are narratives, not implementation manuals. Follow with statistics, market microstructure, portfolio construction, and execution texts if technical competence is the goal.

## Book roles and priority ranges

Use chapter boundaries because editions and ebook pagination vary.

| Book | Required range | Skimmable when rushed | Concepts to extract |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Liar's Poker* | Preface, Chapters 1-11, Epilogue | None on a first pass | sales-trader-client loop, spread, inventory, bonus incentives |
| *Barbarians at the Gate* | Introduction, Prologue, Chapters 1-18 | Chapters 2-3 are background-heavy | LBO, financing, auction, board, management conflict, control |
| *Den of Thieves* | Prologue, Books One and Two, Epilogue | Character detail may be skimmed | information as asset, merger arbitrage, enforcement |
| *More Money Than God* | Introduction, Chapters 1-16, Conclusion | Appendices | macro, long-short, arbitrage, Tiger, LTCM, quant, crisis performance |
| *When Genius Failed* | Introduction, Chapters 1-10, Epilogue | None | convergence, leverage, VaR limits, liquidity spiral, rescue |
| *The Big Short* | Prologue, Chapters 1-10, Epilogue | None | thesis expression, CDS, counterparty, carry, catalyst |
| *The Man Who Solved the Market* | Introduction, Prologue, Chapters 1-16, Epilogue | Early biography may be accelerated | data, signals, ensembles, sizing, execution, secrecy |
| *The Caesars Palace Coup* | Prologue, Parts I-IV, Epilogue | Character background | entity structure, collateral, priority, creditor coalitions, settlement |

Use supplementary material after the relevant book:

- a Bill Gross profile after *Liar's Poker* for duration, bond portfolio management, and risk sizing;
- an LTCM case deck after *When Genius Failed* for convergence trades, VaR, and leverage review;
- a current global-risk report at the end, read selectively for macro context rather than as a Wall Street primer.

## Study outputs

Assign one artifact per stage:

1. *Liar's Poker*: draw the sales-trader-client-spread-bonus loop.
2. *Barbarians at the Gate*: create sources-and-uses plus stakeholder incentives.
3. *Den of Thieves*: separate lawful research, material nonpublic information, and enforcement evidence.
4. *More Money Than God*: compare strategies by edge, horizon, liquidity, and failure mode.
5. *When Genius Failed*: reconstruct the leverage and liquidity feedback loop.
6. *The Big Short*: write a one-page trade memo including carry and counterparty risk.
7. *The Man Who Solved the Market*: map data-to-signal-to-portfolio-to-execution.
8. *The Caesars Palace Coup*: build an entity-level capital-structure and control-rights table.

After the route, synthesize one map:

`investment bank -> PE -> hedge fund -> market maker -> LBO -> leverage -> derivatives -> distressed debt -> activist/M&A -> exit`
