# Chapter 15: Integrated Capital-Game Analysis

**Primary sources:** All sources in this skill

## Core Idea

Analyze Wall Street situations as linked systems of cash, risk, incentives, contracts, control, and time. A transaction can benefit one layer while weakening another, and the decisive leverage may be financial, informational, contractual, or procedural.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Six-map method**
  - When to use: Any complex trade, acquisition, or restructuring.
  - How: Build actor, cash-flow, risk, capital-structure, control-right, and timeline maps.
- **Mechanism-incentive-risk-lesson stack**
  - When to use: Explain a case without collapsing into narrative.
  - How: State how it works, why each actor participates, how it can fail, and what generalizes.
- **Value-realization gate**
  - When to use: A thesis identifies theoretical value.
  - How: Specify catalyst, legal/contractual path, funding survival, decision-maker, and exit distribution.

## Key Concepts

- **Financial leverage:** Borrowing or derivatives magnify changes in equity value.
- **Legal leverage:** Priority, collateral, covenant, or litigation rights improve bargaining position.
- **Information leverage:** Better lawful information or analysis changes decisions.
- **Procedural leverage:** Deadlines, votes, approval rights, and process design alter outcomes.
- **Reflexivity:** Participant actions change the system being analyzed.

## Mental Models

- Follow cash to understand economics, rights to understand power, and incentives to understand behavior.
- Ask what must remain liquid and solvent until the catalyst arrives.
- Separate enterprise value creation from redistribution among claimants.

## Anti-patterns

- **Single-layer analysis:** Valuation without contracts, or contracts without cash flow, misses the game.
- **Static counterparties:** Banks, funds, management, and regulators react to each other.
- **Outcome bias:** A profitable result can come from fragile process; a loss can follow sound process under uncertainty.

## Worked Example

For an LBO that later becomes distressed: map the acquisition sources and uses; identify sponsor and management incentives; track operating cash into debt service; locate collateral and guarantees by entity; model a downturn and refinancing failure; identify the fulcrum claim; map creditor coalitions and litigation; then compare restructuring plans by recovery, control, time, and risk.

## Key Takeaways

1. Build the six maps before taking a strong view.
2. Identify how each participant is paid and what it can force or block.
3. Model path survival and terminal payoff separately.
4. Distinguish value creation from value transfer.
5. Update the map as incentives and control migrate.
