# Chapter 7: Global Macro and Long-Short Judgment

**Primary source:** *More Money Than God*

## Core Idea

Macro and long-short investors turn a thesis into a portfolio while explicitly managing what they do not know. The difficult work is connecting regime, valuation, catalyst, sizing, and exit—not merely predicting direction.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Reflexivity loop**
  - When to use: Market prices influence fundamentals or policy responses.
  - How: Map beliefs -> flows -> prices -> borrower/company behavior -> fundamentals -> revised beliefs.
- **Macro regime table**
  - When to use: Build cross-asset scenarios.
  - How: Define growth, inflation, policy, liquidity, and political states; estimate which exposures benefit or fail in each.
- **Paired-thesis design**
  - When to use: Express security selection while reducing broad market risk.
  - How: Pair a long with a relevant short, then test factor, sector, beta, borrow, and asymmetry rather than assuming the pair is hedged.

## Key Concepts

- **Catalyst:** Event or process that can close the gap between price and thesis.
- **Crowding:** Many investors hold similar positions, increasing correlated exits.
- **Gross exposure:** Sum of long and absolute short exposures.
- **Net exposure:** Long exposure minus short exposure.
- **Short squeeze:** Loss-amplifying rise driven partly by short covering and constrained borrow.

## Mental Models

- Use scenarios to expose dependence on one macro story.
- A hedge should offset the risk you actually own, not merely share a label.
- Treat policy makers and other investors as adaptive participants.

## Anti-patterns

- **Narrative without transmission:** A correct economic view may not imply the chosen asset reaction.
- **False market neutrality:** Low net exposure can coexist with large factor or gross risk.
- **Catalyst-free value:** Cheap assets can remain cheap longer than financing permits.

## Worked Example

Suppose the thesis is that a currency peg is inconsistent with reserves, rates, and domestic politics. Define what breaks the peg, the cost of holding the position, intervention capacity, and what evidence invalidates the view. Size the trade so that delay and adverse intervention do not force exit before the thesis resolves.

## Key Takeaways

1. Specify the transmission mechanism from thesis to instrument.
2. Distinguish gross, net, factor, and tail exposure.
3. Budget for carry and waiting time.
4. Update as market behavior changes the underlying system.
