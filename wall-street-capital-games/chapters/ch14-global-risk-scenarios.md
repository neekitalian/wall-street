# Chapter 14: Global Risk Scenarios

**Primary source:** World Economic Forum, *Global Risks Report 2026*

## Core Idea

Risk reports are scenario inputs, not forecasts or trade signals. Use them to identify interacting regimes, second-order effects, and exposures that historical market data may underrepresent.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Risk transmission map**
  - When to use: A geopolitical, technological, environmental, or social risk may affect portfolios or companies.
  - How: Map event -> policy/behavior -> real economy -> cash flows -> financing -> market prices -> feedback.
- **Horizon split**
  - When to use: Near-term market risk differs from structural risk.
  - How: Separate immediate catalysts, two-year adaptation, and ten-year system change.
- **Compound-risk scenario**
  - When to use: Risks may amplify one another.
  - How: Combine two or three drivers, identify common vulnerabilities, and estimate nonlinear operational and financial effects.

## Key Concepts

- **Multipolarity:** Distribution of power across competing states and blocs.
- **Geoeconomic confrontation:** Use of trade, finance, technology, and sanctions for strategic competition.
- **Infrastructure risk:** Failure or disruption of physical and digital systems.
- **AI systemic risk:** Broad effects from deployment, concentration, misuse, error, and governance gaps.
- **Risk perception survey:** Structured respondent expectations, not an objective probability model.

## Mental Models

- Distinguish risk salience from probability and priced exposure.
- Map second-order transmission before choosing an instrument.
- Use diverse scenarios to challenge portfolio assumptions, not validate a preferred macro narrative.

## Anti-patterns

- **Headline-to-trade leap:** A widely discussed risk may already be priced or lack a clean payoff channel.
- **Single-risk stress:** Real crises often combine funding, policy, operational, and confidence shocks.
- **Survey as forecast:** Respondent rankings contain perspective and sampling limitations.

## Worked Example

Combine geopolitical fragmentation, infrastructure disruption, and AI-enabled cyber risk. Trace effects through supply continuity, insurance, working capital, inflation, rates, credit spreads, and government response. Identify exposures with no redundancy or refinancing cushion. Only then consider hedges and their cost.

## Key Takeaways

1. Translate global risks through causal transmission chains.
2. Separate immediate catalysts from structural adaptation.
3. Stress interacting risks and common dependencies.
4. Do not assume an important risk creates an attractive trade.
