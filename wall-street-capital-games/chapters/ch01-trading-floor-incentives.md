# Chapter 1: Trading-Floor Incentives

**Primary source:** *Liar's Poker* by Michael Lewis

## Core Idea

A trading floor is an incentive system before it is a collection of financial instruments. Salespeople manage clients, traders price and warehouse risk, and management allocates capital and status; compensation converts those roles into behavior.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Sales-trader-client loop**
  - When to use: Analyze who earns a spread and who ultimately owns the position.
  - How: Map client need -> salesperson framing -> trader quote -> execution -> inventory hedge -> revenue attribution -> bonus.
- **Inventory-transfer test**
  - When to use: A bank strongly recommends a security or structure.
  - How: Ask whether the institution is creating exposure, intermediating exposure, or trying to move existing inventory.
- **Principal-agent ladder**
  - When to use: Employee incentives appear inconsistent with the client or firm.
  - How: Compare the interests of employee, desk, firm, client, and capital provider separately.

## Key Concepts

- **Bid-ask spread:** Compensation for supplying immediacy and accepting inventory or information risk.
- **Revenue credit:** Internal attribution that affects compensation even when legal profit belongs to the firm.
- **Adverse selection:** The risk that the counterparty trades because it knows more.
- **Inventory:** Positions held between client trades or while risk is being redistributed.
- **Bonus pool:** A mechanism that turns short-period revenue into individual incentives.

## Mental Models

- Follow compensation when an organizational decision looks irrational.
- Treat a quote as both a price and an offer to transfer risk.
- Separate a product's usefulness from the seller's reason for offering it now.

## Anti-patterns

- **Assuming advice is neutral:** A salesperson can be helpful while still serving the dealer's inventory and revenue objectives.
- **Equating desk P&L with customer value:** Profitable intermediation does not prove that the client received a good risk-adjusted outcome.

## Worked Example

A client asks for a mortgage bond. Sales obtains a trader quote. The trader may already own similar bonds and prices the trade to reduce inventory while preserving spread. Sales presents the instrument in terms relevant to the client. The analytical task is not to assume misconduct; it is to record who holds the bond before and after, what the alternatives were, and how the desk and salesperson are paid.

## Key Takeaways

1. Map roles and incentives before evaluating the stated market view.
2. Ask where risk sits after every transaction.
3. Revenue attribution explains internal politics and product emphasis.
4. A bank can simultaneously provide liquidity and seek to transfer unwanted risk.
