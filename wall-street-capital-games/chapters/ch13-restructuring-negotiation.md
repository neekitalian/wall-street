# Chapter 13: Restructuring Negotiation and Creditor Coalitions

**Primary source:** *The Caesars Palace Coup*

## Core Idea

Restructuring is a repeated bargaining game under legal deadlines. Parties trade valuation positions, litigation claims, new money, releases, timing, and control; nominal priority is the starting point, not the whole settlement.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **BATNA-by-class**
  - When to use: Compare proposed settlements.
  - How: For each stakeholder estimate recovery, delay, cost, control, and risk if no agreement is reached.
- **Coalition map**
  - When to use: Claims are dispersed across funds and tranches.
  - How: Record holdings, blocking positions, shared advisers, information agreements, conflicts, and incentives to defect.
- **Litigation-adjusted recovery**
  - When to use: Claims against sponsors, directors, or transfers influence bargaining.
  - How: Estimate potential award, probability, time, cost, collectability, and effect on plan confirmation.

## Key Concepts

- **Restructuring support agreement:** Contract documenting stakeholder support subject to terms and milestones.
- **Cramdown:** Confirmation over dissent when statutory requirements are satisfied.
- **Release:** Waiver of claims, often exchanged for value or plan support.
- **New money:** Financing that can receive priority and shift bargaining power.
- **Examiner:** Court-appointed investigator in specified bankruptcy circumstances.

## Mental Models

- Value time and certainty alongside headline recovery.
- A legal claim matters partly because it changes the other side's BATNA.
- Coalition stability depends on distribution within the group, not just total group recovery.

## Anti-patterns

- **One-creditor-class fiction:** Holders in the same broad category can own different instruments and incentives.
- **Litigation at face value:** Large alleged damages are not the same as expected collectible value.
- **Ignoring milestones:** Deadlines can create option value or force bad settlements.

## Worked Example

Senior creditors favor a rapid plan with cash and new equity; junior creditors threaten litigation over asset transfers. Compare each class's recovery under settlement versus years of litigation. Model how an examiner report changes probability, delay, and reputational cost. A settlement can exceed strict expected litigation value because it buys confirmation certainty and releases.

## Key Takeaways

1. Quantify each class's no-deal alternative.
2. Map blocking rights and coalition fractures.
3. Convert legal claims into probability-weighted bargaining value.
4. Track who supplies new money and what priority or control it receives.
5. Treat process timing as an economic variable.
