# Chapter 2: Bond Market-Making and Portfolio Risk

**Primary sources:** *Liar's Poker*; Carol J. Loomis's Bill Gross profile

## Core Idea

Fixed-income markets combine contractual cash flows with unstable prices, funding, and human expectations. Dealers monetize distribution and inventory; portfolio managers monetize security selection, duration, relative value, and disciplined sizing.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Duration-first analysis**
  - When to use: Compare bonds with different maturities or rate sensitivity.
  - How: Estimate the price effect of a rate change, then test credit spread, convexity, and liquidity rather than treating yield as the whole return.
- **Singles-and-doubles sizing**
  - When to use: An investor has an apparent edge but uncertain timing.
  - How: Limit any single exposure, diversify independent return sources, and add risk only as evidence improves.
- **Product-innovation cycle**
  - When to use: A new security grows rapidly.
  - How: Trace the underlying borrower cash flows, structuring transformation, dealer distribution, investor demand, hedging market, and incentive to increase volume.

## Key Concepts

- **Duration:** Approximate sensitivity of a bond's price to interest-rate changes.
- **Convexity:** Curvature that makes duration's linear approximation change as yields move.
- **Spread:** Yield compensation above a reference curve for credit, liquidity, optionality, and uncertainty.
- **Mortgage-backed security:** Claims on mortgage cash flows with prepayment and extension behavior.
- **Relative value:** Pricing one instrument against a related instrument rather than making a pure directional forecast.

## Mental Models

- Treat certainty of contractual cash flows and uncertainty of market value as separate dimensions.
- Prefer multiple modest edges to one heroic forecast.
- When a product's supply explodes, inspect underwriting and distribution incentives.

## Anti-patterns

- **Yield-only selection:** High yield can be compensation for hidden optionality, illiquidity, or default risk.
- **Model as oracle:** A risk number is conditional on assumptions and cannot eliminate regime change.

## Worked Example

Compare a short-duration Treasury with a long-duration bond. A rate rise may create a much larger price decline in the long bond even if neither defaults. Next add a corporate spread and ask whether the extra yield compensates for default and liquidity. Finally decide position size: a plausible forecast should not justify a portfolio-threatening bet.

## Key Takeaways

1. Decompose bond return into rates, spread, carry, roll, optionality, and credit.
2. Size positions for forecast error and liquidity, not confidence alone.
3. Product growth changes both opportunity and incentive quality.
4. Portfolio construction is a risk-allocation problem, not a collection of favorite securities.
