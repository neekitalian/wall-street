# Chapter 12: Distressed Capital Structure and Entity Mapping

**Primary source:** *The Caesars Palace Coup* by Max Frumes and Sujeet Indap

## Core Idea

When a company is distressed, value alone does not determine recovery. Legal entities, collateral, guarantees, priority, covenants, transfers, and procedural rights decide which claim has leverage over which assets.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Entity-claim map**
  - When to use: Analyze a complex corporate group.
  - How: For every legal entity list assets, cash flows, debt, collateral, guarantees, ownership, and intercompany claims.
- **Recovery waterfall**
  - When to use: Estimate claim value under restructuring or liquidation.
  - How: Estimate distributable value, deduct senior and administrative claims, allocate by legal priority, then test disputes and settlement deviations.
- **Control-through-claims test**
  - When to use: A debt position may influence restructuring.
  - How: Identify voting class, blocking threshold, collateral enforcement, consent right, covenant, maturity, and potential equity conversion.

## Key Concepts

- **Secured debt:** Claim supported by liens on specified collateral.
- **Structural subordination:** Claim at a parent depends on value flowing up after subsidiary creditors.
- **Guarantee:** Promise by another entity to satisfy an obligation.
- **Fulcrum security:** Claim expected to convert into or control reorganized equity at the valuation boundary.
- **Fraudulent transfer:** Transfer potentially challengeable under insolvency and value-related legal tests.

## Mental Models

- Draw the legal entity chart before reading the consolidated balance sheet.
- Separate enterprise value dispute from priority dispute.
- Price a claim as a bundle of recovery, timing, litigation, and control options.

## Anti-patterns

- **Consolidated-company shortcut:** Assets and liabilities may not sit in the same entity.
- **Automatic waterfall:** Settlements, litigation risk, new money, and plan feasibility can alter strict priority outcomes.
- **Debt label equivalence:** Two "senior" bonds can have different collateral and guarantees.

## Worked Example

A parent owns an operating subsidiary. The subsidiary's lenders have liens and guarantees; parent notes are unsecured. Even if consolidated enterprise value exceeds total debt, subsidiary creditors may be paid first from operating assets. Build separate recoveries by entity, then model the parent claim only from residual value or valid guarantees.

## Key Takeaways

1. Map entities, assets, claims, and guarantees before valuation.
2. Identify the fulcrum security under multiple value scenarios.
3. Treat legal rights and litigation as components of claim value.
4. Control can migrate from equity to creditors before formal ownership changes.
