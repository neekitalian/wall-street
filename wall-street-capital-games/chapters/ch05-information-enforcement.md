# Chapter 5: Information, Merger Arbitrage, and Enforcement

**Primary source:** *Den of Thieves* by James B. Stewart

## Core Idea

Information has market value, but its legal use depends on how it was obtained, duties owed, and trading or disclosure conduct. Analyze the information chain and evidence rather than treating every advantage as either genius or crime.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Information provenance chain**
  - When to use: A trade precedes a corporate announcement.
  - How: Map origin -> duty or confidentiality -> intermediaries -> recipient -> trade -> benefit -> concealment or disclosure.
- **Merger-arbitrage payoff tree**
  - When to use: Evaluate a position in an announced or anticipated transaction.
  - How: Model completion price and timing, break price, probabilities, financing/regulatory conditions, borrow, carry, and position size.
- **Evidence mosaic**
  - When to use: Separate suspicious coincidence from provable conduct.
  - How: Combine timing, communications, relationships, trading records, payments, false statements, and attempts to conceal.

## Key Concepts

- **Material nonpublic information:** Information a reasonable investor would consider important that is not broadly public.
- **Merger arbitrage:** Trading the price gap and outcome probabilities around a transaction.
- **Tipper/tippee:** Parties in a chain of disclosure and receipt whose liability depends on legal elements and facts.
- **Market color:** Observations about flows or conditions that may be legitimate but require careful handling.
- **Mosaic research:** Combining lawful public and immaterial pieces into an investment conclusion.

## Mental Models

- Ask what duty travels with information, not merely whether the information is valuable.
- Separate research edge, access edge, and prohibited disclosure.
- In event trades, size from downside and uncertainty rather than confidence in the rumor.

## Anti-patterns

- **Everyone knows:** Repetition does not make nonpublic information public.
- **No written message means no evidence:** Trading, phone, payment, and relationship patterns can be evidence.
- **Outcome proves legality:** A profitable trade says nothing by itself about how the information was obtained.

## Worked Example

An analyst infers a possible acquisition from public filings, industry checks, and option prices. Contrast that with receiving a confidential board decision from an adviser and trading before announcement. Both may lead to the same thesis; provenance, duty, and conduct make them legally different. For an actual question, consult current counsel and jurisdiction-specific law.

## Key Takeaways

1. Document sources and restrictions on sensitive information.
2. Model event-trade downside independently of the information thesis.
3. Do not collapse legal analysis into moral intuition or market custom.
4. Information controls protect both market integrity and the institution.
