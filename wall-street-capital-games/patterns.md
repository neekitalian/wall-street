# Patterns

## Six-Map Case Analysis

**When to use:** A trade or transaction has multiple institutions, claims, or stages.

**How:** Create actor, cash-flow, risk, capital-structure, control-right, and timeline maps. Reconcile inconsistencies before forming a conclusion.

**Trade-offs:** Comprehensive but slower; use a one-page first pass, then expand decisive areas.

## Sales-Trader-Client Loop

**When to use:** Analyze a dealer-distributed instrument.

**How:** Record client objective, sales framing, trader inventory, quote, hedge, spread, revenue attribution, and post-trade risk owner.

**Trade-offs:** Clarifies incentives but does not by itself establish suitability or misconduct.

## LBO Underwriting Bridge

**When to use:** Evaluate a leveraged acquisition.

**How:** Build sources and uses; forecast cash flow and debt; decompose returns into operations, deleveraging, and multiple change; stress downturn and refinancing.

**Trade-offs:** Transparent but sensitive to operating and exit assumptions.

## Bid-Quality Matrix

**When to use:** Compare competing acquisition proposals.

**How:** Score price, financing certainty, conditions, timing, regulatory exposure, liabilities, and remedies; overlay stakeholder conflicts.

**Trade-offs:** Requires subjective probabilities but is superior to headline-price ranking.

## Information Provenance Chain

**When to use:** Sensitive information informs a trade.

**How:** Trace source, confidentiality or duty, intermediaries, recipient, action, benefit, disclosure, and evidence. Escalate fact-specific legal questions.

**Trade-offs:** Protects process but cannot replace legal advice.

## Strategy Fingerprint

**When to use:** Compare hedge funds or strategies.

**How:** Record edge, universe, gross/net exposure, horizon, liquidity, leverage, capacity, catalyst, and failure mode.

**Trade-offs:** Forces comparability while simplifying strategy-specific detail.

## Convergence Trade Stress

**When to use:** A relative-value spread looks unusually wide.

**How:** Model widening before convergence, hedge error, crowding, haircuts, margin calls, liquidation depth, and counterparty responses.

**Trade-offs:** May reject profitable trades; protects against terminally correct but path-insolvent positions.

## One-Page Trade Memo

**When to use:** Before allocating meaningful risk.

**How:** State thesis, market view, instrument, payoff, catalyst, horizon, carry, liquidity, financing, counterparty, size, invalidation, and exit.

**Trade-offs:** Cannot capture every nuance, but prevents thesis drift and hidden assumptions.

## Data-to-Execution Pipeline

**When to use:** Assess a quantitative strategy.

**How:** Audit data, feature, signal, validation, portfolio, constraint, order, fill, attribution, and monitoring stages.

**Trade-offs:** Strong governance adds latency but reduces research and operational failure.

## Entity-Claim Map

**When to use:** A corporate group is distressed.

**How:** List assets, cash flows, debt, collateral, guarantees, ownership, and intercompany claims for every entity; build scenario recoveries.

**Trade-offs:** Labor intensive but essential where structural subordination matters.

## BATNA-by-Class

**When to use:** Evaluate a restructuring settlement.

**How:** For every class compare recovery, timing, litigation cost, control, and risk under agreement versus no deal.

**Trade-offs:** Probability estimates are uncertain; makes negotiation assumptions explicit.

## Compound-Risk Scenario

**When to use:** Macro, geopolitical, technology, and funding risks may interact.

**How:** Map joint shock transmission through operations, cash flow, financing, prices, and feedback; identify common dependencies and mitigations.

**Trade-offs:** Avoid false precision; use scenarios to expose fragility rather than predict dates.
